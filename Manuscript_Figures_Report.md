# NorHA Matrix Stiffness and Brain Organoid Development

## Results

Preliminary studies showed differences in brain organoid morphology between NorHA and Matrigel from day 7 to day 19. On day 35, MAP2 staining extended across and within Matrigel-grown organoids. In NorHA-grown organoids, staining was concentrated around the surface. Immunofluorescence images also showed different staining patterns across DTT concentrations at days 64 and 94 (slide 4).

Mechanical characterization showed increasing storage modulus with increasing crosslinking percentage. The 50% DS formulation had a higher storage modulus than the 34% DS formulation at 2.5% and 5% crosslinking. Matrigel was labeled as approximately 73 Pa (slide 5).

At 31% DS and 21 Pa, day 94 organoid area, perimeter, and circularity showed no significant differences from Matrigel. ASCL1 expression was lower and marked with two asterisks. The other gene expression plots had no significance annotations. SOX2-, TUJ1-, and MAP2-positive area measurements were presented without significance annotations (slide 6).

At day 10, organoid area was lower at 40, 73, and 102 Pa than in Matrigel. The reported p-values were 0.014, 0.049, and 0.032, respectively. Perimeter and circularity comparisons were labeled nonsignificant. At day 94, area and perimeter were lower at 40 and 102 Pa than in Matrigel, with both comparisons marked by two asterisks. The 73 Pa comparisons were labeled nonsignificant. Circularity comparisons remained nonsignificant (slide 7).

NeuN expression was higher at 40 Pa than in Matrigel (p = 0.0344). The 102 Pa condition had the highest plotted mean MAP2 and SYNP relative expression in this stiffness series. These increases had no significance annotations. MAP2-positive area was higher at 73 Pa than in Matrigel and marked with one asterisk. The corresponding 40 and 102 Pa comparisons were labeled nonsignificant (slide 8).

At 50% DS and 21 Pa, day 90 organoid area, perimeter, and circularity showed no significant differences from Matrigel. MAP2, NeuN, and SYNP expression were higher than in Matrigel. MAP2 was marked with two asterisks. NeuN and SYNP were each marked with one asterisk. SOX2 and ASCL1 had no significance annotations. Mean TUJ1- and MAP2-positive areas were higher, but these plots had no significance annotations (slide 9).

At 1817 Pa, plotted ASCL1, MAP2, NeuN, and SOX2 relative expression exceeded the Matrigel reference. SYNP was below the reference. Calcium recordings showed changes in GCaMP intensity over time in both conditions. The plotted NorHA measurements had lower spike frequency and higher amplitude than the Matrigel measurement. These panels provided no significance annotations (slide 10).

The qPCR captions on slides 8 and 9 specify Welch's one-way ANOVA on biological-replicate ΔCt values, followed by Dunnett's T3 comparisons against Matrigel. Relative expression was displayed as 2^(-ΔΔCt), with mean ± SEM and n = 3. Asterisk counts are retained as shown because their thresholds are not defined in the presentation. Missing annotations are not treated as evidence of nonsignificance.

## Conclusions and Proposed Project Direction

My assessment is that 50% DS at 21 Pa is a strong candidate for follow-up. It shows coordinated increases in MAP2, NeuN, and SYNP expression, with significance annotations for all three. The current presentation does not establish faster maturation or an optimal stiffness.

I recommend confirming this condition in independent biological replicates, with each condition compared against Matrigel. Include matched culture times and protein quantification. Extend the calcium measurements to the same conditions to assess whether expression differences accompany functional differences.

To examine stiffness specifically, vary stiffness while holding DS and the DTT/MMP ratio constant. Testing between 102 Pa and 1 kPa is a reasonable next experiment, but the proposed optimum remains a hypothesis. Before selecting conditions, clarify the 31% and 34% DS labels and complete the statistical annotations. This would strengthen the connection between matrix formulation, organoid growth, and maturation measurements.
